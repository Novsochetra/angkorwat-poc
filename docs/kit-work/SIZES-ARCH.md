# Real-world sizes for the architecture kit (sections 15, 16, 17, 21)

One shared size table for every builder of §15 (walls), §16 (terraces), §17 (moat and
water) and §21 (voxel building kit). 1 unit = 1 m, the explorer is 1.70 m and climbs
ledges up to 0.42 m, and the texel is 1/16 m. Sizes are chosen to land on 0.125 m or 0.25 m
where possible.

**Confidence:** **M** = measured or published figure (source in §4). **P** = estimate from
photos, scaled against a person, a devata relief (0.95–1.1 m), a 1.37 m baluster window or
a stone course. **E** = estimate by reasoning. Anything marked P or E is an estimate.

A single 1 × 2 × 0.5 m stone would weigh ≈ 2.3 t, more than Angkor Wat's 1.5 t maximum, so
anything bigger than about 1.5 × 0.5 × 0.6 m must show joints (several stones).

### 1.2 Horizontal rhythm
- **2.0 m bay** is the kit's base rhythm: gallery pillars (≈ 1.9 m apart centre to centre, P), naga
  balustrade posts and causeway columns (≈ 2 m apart, E), and rows of close-set windows (P).
- **3.0 m bay** for façades whose windows sit between wide piers carved with devatas (P).
- Module lengths: multiples of 2 m, or 6 m when 2 m and 3 m bays are mixed.

### 1.4 Doorways

surround (colonnettes and pilasters) about 3 times the clear width, under a **0.5 m** lintel (P).

### 1.5 Baluster windows
Measured on two windows at 2 : 1 scale against devatas and a doorway (P):
- Clear opening **1.375 m wide × 1.5–1.75 m high** (measured 1.37 × 1.50 m and 1.37 × 1.79 m). Kit: 1.375 × 1.625 m.
- Moulded outer frame ≈ **0.1875 m** wide on each side (outer size ≈ 1.75 × 2.0 m).
- Sill ≈ **0.5 m** above the gallery floor (measured 0.54 m). The head sits ≈ 0.25 m above a door's head.
- **7 balusters** (some windows have 5), spaced 0.19 m centre to centre, so they nearly touch.
  Each is ≈ 0.17 m across at the rings and 0.10 m at the necks. Kit: 0.1875 m spacing, 0.125 m
  core, 0.1875 m rings. They stand the full clear height.
- Windows onto the dark interior are deep recesses. Some are blind (false) windows with the
  balusters half-sunk into the wall (E).

### 1.7 Galleries
- Clear corridor widths from Glaize: outer west-entrance galleries 2.20 m; first-level (bas-relief)
  gallery 2.45 m; second-enclosure gallery 2.45 m (N/S cloister galleries 2.90 m); cruciform
  cloister nave 3.15 m (7.70 m with its double aisles); upper (Bakan) gallery 2.0 m, its axial
  naves 2.40 m (**M**).
- Kit: **2.5 m** clear corridor (2.0 m on the Bakan) between 1.0 m walls, so 4.5 m outside. Add
  the outer pillared half-gallery (a lean-to aisle) for **1.75–2.0 m** clear (P) with its
  0.5 m pillars on the 2 m bay. The half-gallery roof is ≈ 1.5 m lower than the main ridge (E).
- Bas-relief band: **2.0 m** high on the inner wall of the first gallery (M), starting ≈ 1 m above the floor (E).

### 1.11 Guardian lions
- Angkor Wat lions **stand** on four straight legs with the chest raised and the head up. They do not sit (P).
- Lion **1.3–1.5 m** from feet to top of the mane, on a **0.25–0.4 m** two-tier plinth; ≈ 1.6–1.9 m in all, about the explorer's height. Footprint ≈ 0.6 × 1.0 m (P/E).
- Kit: **1.375 m lion + 0.375 m plinth**.
- Loose Khmer guardian lions in museums are smaller (0.81 m UMMA, 1.09 m Met, M). Late Bayon-period ones reach 2 m.

### 1.13 Gopura (west entrance)
- 235 m colonnade (Glaize); 240 × 35 m (Hello Angkor). Three central gates with cruciform
  towers (their tops are ruined), with ground-level "elephant gates" at both ends (**M**).
- Porch plinth ≈ 1.5 m; portico columns ≈ 3.4 m; porch door ≈ 2.9 m high (P). Surviving towers ≈ 18–22 m (E, low).

### 1.14 Causeway (west, across the moat)
- **200 m × 12 m** (Glaize; some sources give 250 m including the end terraces) (M). Sandstone-paved.
- A cruciform "lion terrace" at the west end and a landing at the gopura end. The Sophia
  restoration speaks of a "terrace" 100 m from the end, so there may be a landing midway (unconfirmed).
- **Solid earth fill**, not a bridge. Its sides are laterite retaining walls with round sandstone
  columns (⌀ ≈ 0.55 m, ≈ 2 m apart, E) standing in the water against the wall. The columns carry
  a corbelled sandstone edge band ≈ 0.5 m high, projecting ≈ 0.3–0.5 m, with the naga balustrade on top (M description / P sizes).
- Deck ≈ **3.4 m above the dry-season water** (P).

## 3. What the sheets imply vs reality

- **Stones are cubes on the sheets.** Real Angkor Wat ashlars are long: 0.45–0.5 m high, 0.6–0.65 m deep and 1–1.2 m or more long, laid in running bond. `FACADE.course = 0.25` is half the real course height. Use 0.5 m courses for wall bodies and 0.25 m only for moulding bands, or record in the `size` note why the look needs 0.25.
- **Too few balusters.** The sheets draw 2–4 per window (§15.7: three windows of ≈ 3; §21.2 window: 4). Real windows have **7** (sometimes 5), set so close they nearly touch, in a ≈ 1.4 × 1.5–1.8 m opening. `gallery.ts` defaults to one per 0.45 m, which gives 3 on a 1.5 m window. It should give 7 (0.1875 m spacing).
- **Doors too narrow for their height.** The doors in §15.6 and §21.2 are ≈ 3 : 1 and cut plainly into the wall. Real doors are ≈ 2 : 1, framed by colonnettes and pilasters about 3 times the opening's width, under a 0.5 m carved lintel.
- **Walls too low.** Wall thickness on the sheets (2 blocks ≈ 1 m; the thick wall 3 blocks) is fine. But the walls stand only about 6 courses (≈ 3 m). Real gallery walls reach ≈ 5 m to the cornice, plus 2–2.75 m of stone roof.
- **Pillars too squat.** §21.2 draws the pillar ≈ 3 × as tall as it is wide. Real gallery pillars are ≈ 6–7 × (0.5 m square, 3–3.5 m tall).
- **Stairs:**
  - §21.2 has ≈ 0.5 m (one-block) risers, above the explorer's 0.42 m limit; real risers are 0.2–0.3 m.
  - Temple stairs are steeper and narrower than drawn. The 70° Bakan stairs (40 steps) appear on no sheet.
  - §16.6 is fine if its steps are 0.25 m.
- **Terraces far too low.** The sheets stack 3–5 courses (≈ 1.5–2.5 m). The real levels are 1.5 m (avenue), 3.3 m (first level), +5.8 m (second level) and +11–13 m (Bakan), each a tall moulded base with few, steep stairs.
- **Parapet is not Angkor Wat.** §16.7 draws a post-and-rail parapet ≈ 2 m tall with 3 m end posts. Angkor Wat's terrace edges carry the **naga balustrade**, 1.0 m high (a round body on short posts), or nothing.
- **Naga (§21.2 #10):**
  - Sheet: one dragon-like head on a solid two-course plinth.
  - Real: a **seven-headed cobra fan** ≈ 1.3–1.5 m wide, rising ≈ 2 m on its own post, 2.5–3 m above the deck.
  - The body is round (⌀ ≈ 0.4 m) and carried on open short posts ≈ 2 m apart, so you can see between them.
- **Lion (§21.2 #11):** the sheet's lion sits on its haunches. Angkor Wat's lions stand on four legs, chest up: 1.3–1.5 m on a 0.25–0.4 m plinth, about as tall as the explorer.
- **Causeway (§21.3 #6):**
  - Sheet: a narrow bridge on rectangular piers with water flowing under it.
  - Real: a **12 m wide, 200 m long solid earth-filled causeway**. Laterite side walls, round sandstone columns in front of them carrying a corbelled edge, deck ≈ 3.4 m above the water.
  - The sheet's deck is also far too narrow compared with its balustrades.
- **Moat (§21.3 #7, §17.1 #3):**
  - Sheet: a small closed tank with vertical walls 3–4 cube courses high and posts at the corners.
  - Real: the moat is 190 m wide. Its revetment is a flight of ≈ 10 low steps (0.3 m, laterite below, sandstone above) under a moulded 0.5 m coping. It is not a vertical wall, and there are no corner posts.
  - Only the central west stretch is stepped; elsewhere the banks are grassy.
- **Lily pads (§17.2 #3):** the sheet's pads are four-lobed like clover. Real water-lily pads are round with a single V-notch, 0.2–0.4 m across, with 0.15 m flowers sitting just above the water. Lotus leaves (if used) stand up out of the water instead of floating.
- **Terrace and tower sections (§21.3 #4, #5):**
  - Sheets: mini towers on the terrace corners. Angkor Wat terraces end in lions or naga fans (E).
  - Real towers: a body, 5 receding tiers and a lotus-bud crown. The corner towers rise ≈ 70% as high as the central one above the Bakan.
- **Building kit 2x4 (§21.1 #5):** as one stone it would be ≈ 2.3 t, more than Angkor Wat's 1.5 t maximum. Show the joints.
